# Numerical data and figure provenance

`prepared.js` is a static authoring output generated by `../../scripts/prepare_data.py`. The browser loads it as a local script; no network computation or solver endpoint is used.

## Inventory expectations

- Model: manuscript §4.1, single-product inventory with partial backlog and lost sales.
- State grid: 81 equally spaced values on [−4,12].
- Order grid: 41 equally spaced values on [0,6].
- Demand: Normal(6,3.5²) truncated to [0,10].
- Expectation: 256-node Gauss–Legendre quadrature, weighted by the truncated density.
- Stored quantities: expected period cost, expected next state, and expected squared next state.
- Costs: purchase 20, holding 1, backlog 25, disposal 5, lost sales 3000; discount 0.95.

Browser calculations rearrange the quadratic Bellman inequality into β₀ ≤ f, inspect the violation threshold, and normalize exp(−f/λ) over the display grid. Grid minima and normalized grid weights are illustrations, not certified continuous optimization or integration results. The heatmap's darker color means smaller f, while violation depends on the separately displayed intercept β₀.

## Delayed allocation

- Instance: `code/Code/weakly-coupled-mdp/notebooks/text_example.ipynb`, matching the manuscript's three-product, two-decision-period example.
- Implementation: supplied `wmdp.py` and `delayedallocation.py`, imported without modifying those files.
- Solver: Gurobi 13.0.1 with default tolerances; exact pricing over the five feasible joint actions.
- Initial support: only 000 in each period; a zero-reward terminal period is included by the implementation.
- Recorded fields: restricted LP objective, represented actions, positive weights, dual prices, all candidate reduced costs, and exact deterministic-policy reward.
- Verification: restricted objectives reproduce 0, 23.5, 49, 49; exact terminal pricing finds no positive reduced cost in either decision period.

Intermediate restricted objectives are not automatically bounds on the original optimal policy reward. The replay is a prepared solve and is labeled accordingly. A solver may return different dual solutions in degenerate cases; corresponding action/price traces can differ while retaining the validated objective sequence.

## Continuous-method result figures

The PNGs in `../figures/` are unmodified copies from `text/latex/figs/`. They show the manuscript's constraint landscape, Gibbs densities, PSMD snapshots, fixed-basis outcomes, and guided/unguided feature outcomes. The supplied previous QMD describes its own live results as teaching estimates and its lightweight PSMD code as a surrogate. Those outputs are not silently substituted for the manuscript's figures. Speaker notes retain the distinction between theorem conditions, full-domain constraints, sampled enforcement, simulation estimates, and actual policy performance.

## Outdoor display with a knapsack budget (slides 12–22)

`assortment.js` is generated by `scripts/prepare_assortment.py` using the supplied
`delayedallocation.py` with Gurobi 13.0.1. This teaching instance is separate from
the manuscript's resource example. It has two decision periods, three binary
feature decisions, and a display-space budget of 8 units.

| Product | Space | Low-type profit | High-type profit | Prior expected profit |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Backpack | 2 | $2 | $10 | $6 |
| Cooler | 3 | $4 | $12 | $8 |
| Tent | 6 | $0 | $24 | $12 |

Product demand types are independent and persistent. Each is initially low or
high with equal probability. Featuring a product reveals its type through sales;
a product not featured stays unknown. This perfect-signal assumption makes the
learning example small and explicit. Rewards are incremental profit from the
display, with zero reward when not featured and no terminal reward.

The six feasible displays are empty, each singleton, backpack+cooler, and
backpack+tent. Initial support is empty, backpack+cooler, tent only. Its LP
objective is 31.5. Exact pricing adds backpack+tent in both periods, with scores
7.5 and 5; re-solving gives 40, with no positive pricing score remaining.

The displayed randomized policy samples one full assortment from each period's
optimized weights, independently of the observed state. Initial weights are
0.5/0.5 on backpack+cooler/tent in period 0 and 0.75/0.25 in period 1. Refined
weights are 1 on backpack+tent in period 0 and 0.5/0.5 on backpack+cooler and
backpack+tent in period 1. Exact evaluation over demand types and policy draws
gives **26.5 → 34**. Independent Bellman enumeration gives an optimal value of
40. The LP objective is not reported as the sampled policy's achieved profit.
Selecting a highest-weight action would instead be a deterministic policy.

The independent SciPy helper `assets/python/delayed_allocation.py` uses the same flow equations and supports additional per-product display costs. Default objectives and policy profits match the Gurobi trace. With tent display cost 25, the optimum is 28 (backpack+cooler in both periods). High costs on all products give the empty-display optimum 0. Local tests and actual Pyodide runs check these reference cases. Degeneracy can change weights without changing objective values.

The script validates probability normalization, LP residuals, knapsack feasibility,
exact pricing termination, and the direction of objective changes. Slide 16 uses
actual initial-LP backpack flows: period-1 feature probabilities are 0.5 for
unknown, 0.25 for known high, and 0 for known low; the remaining 0.25 is known low
and not featured. Their feature sum 0.75 agrees with the assortment weight.

The earlier two-slot demand-development instance and greedy policy are superseded.
All active slide text, examples, sampling controls, and checks use this weighted
budget and demand-learning model. Regenerate data and update QMD values together
when changing model inputs. Slides 12–22 use prepared results; slide 23 now performs a real browser LP solve.
