What is Sequential Decision Making?

Sequential decision making involves choices over time that affect future realizations and costs.

Retailing

Order today; carry stock into tomorrow.

Autonomous delivery

Choose each route as new obstacles appear.

Energy storage

Store energy now to serve demand later.

Cire · Nadarajah · Pakiman · Soheili

Each application connects a choice now to the information and resources available for the next choice. In retailing, an order changes the stock available after uncertain demand. A delivery robot chooses a route, observes its surroundings, and chooses again from its new location. A battery operator decides when to charge or discharge; the remaining energy limits later choices as renewable supply, demand, and prices change.

First advance reveals the MDP row. Construct builds the representation and optimization model. Optimize solves that model and provides information used to extract a feasible policy. For delayed allocation, these are component flows and feasible joint action weights. For value function approximation, the fitted coefficients can induce a policy through an action optimization. Policy extraction is method dependent and its quality must be assessed. Refine evaluates quality using an appropriate bound or policy evaluation and feeds information back to Construct. Section 2.3 gives three examples: enriching the basis, tightening the model, or adjusting the state relevance distribution. These are different ways to update the approximation; Refine is not limited to adding features or enlarging a representation. Delayed allocation adds useful feasible joint actions; self guided ALPs can add basis functions and guiding constraints. Second advance closes the loop across solves. Not every method requires all three stages, and a model does not have to be revised after every successful solve.

The definition applies to automatic choices in one or more stages, not to every modeling choice in the table. A manually repeated workflow alone is not what makes the approximation self adapting. The tutorial describes adaptation to problem structure, instance data or solution progress, with the aim of reducing user burden and retaining useful performance. In the later delayed allocation example the LP solution guides which omitted feasible assortments to add. That is an example of adaptation in Refine, not the definition of all adaptation.

These are complementary computational tools. Mathematical programming exploits formulation structure, first-order methods provide inexpensive iterative updates, and sampling supports both optimization and approximation construction. The categories overlap: constraint-violation learning combines primal-dual stochastic mirror descent with adaptive sampling, so Lin, Nadarajah, and Soheili (2020) appears in both columns. This is a capability map, not an invention or adoption timeline.

For mathematical programming, self-adapting network relaxations compress the representation of feasible decisions while retaining linking constraints. Delayed allocation maintains a restricted support of feasible joint actions and expands it through pricing. Their bounds and policy guarantees depend on the conditions established in the respective papers.

For first-order methods, constraint-violation learning fits value-function coefficients through primal-dual stochastic mirror descent. The weakly time-coupled approximation reorganizes temporal dependence to support parallel block-coordinate optimization. Its horizon-independent complexity result relies on the paper’s formulation and assumptions; it is not a general property of all first-order methods.

For sampling, constraint-violation learning samples informative state-action pairs from a density adapted to constraint violations, replacing repeated global separation with targeted sampling. Sampling this density can itself be difficult in high dimensions. Self-guided ALPs sample random features and enrich the approximation across solves, reducing manual basis design. They still require solving the resulting ALPs; random-feature sampling alone is not an optimization algorithm. Representation, optimization, and policy-quality guarantees remain distinct.

Foundation models remain an open direction, as discussed in Tutorial §5. Possible roles span model construction, interpretation of solver output, refinement choices, and approximate subproblem oracles. How to integrate them with bounds and guarantees remains open. Assess any benefits using the user-effort, computation, and decision-quality criteria on the next slide.
